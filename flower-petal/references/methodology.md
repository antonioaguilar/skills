# Flower Petal Methodology

The "Flower Petal" exercise (based on the Technology-Application Matrix or "Core and Petals" mapping) helps R&D labs, startup founders, and product strategists prevent "product-market fit blinders."

## The Concept: Core vs. Petals

Do not mistake your current product for your actual core asset.

### The Core (The Center of the Flower)
The core is not a product; it is your underlying, proprietary technical capability or unique IP. It is usually described as an action, an algorithm, a material property, or a scientific process.
- **Bad Core definition**: "We built an app that tracks delivery trucks." (That is an application)
- **Good Core definition**: "A low-latency, predictive geospatial routing algorithm." (That is the core technology)

### The Petals (The Applications)
Each petal represents a distinct market, use case, or industry vertical where that exact core capability solves a high-value problem.

## How to Run the Flower Petal Exercise

### Step 1 - Strip the Technology to its Naked Core
Define your technology without mentioning your current product or target customer. Ask: "If we stripped away the user interface and the specific data we use today, what does the engine actually do at a physics, mathematical, or mechanical level?"

### Step 2 - Brainstorm the 'Unrelated' Petals
Look at the core capability and ask: "Who else desperately needs this exact mechanism?" Brainstorm different industries or use cases.

### Step 3 - Filter by Market Friction
Evaluate each petal against market realities (regulatory hurdles, time-to-market, competitors).

### Step 4 - Select Your Beachhead Petal
Pick the one petal that offers the lowest barrier to entry and the highest immediate value.
