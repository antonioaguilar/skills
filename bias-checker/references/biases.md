# Cognitive Biases Reference

This list is based on [The Decision Lab](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases).

*   **Action Bias:** The tendency to prefer doing something over doing nothing, even when there is no evidence that action will lead to a better outcome.
*   **Affect Heuristic:** Relying on current emotions to make quick decisions rather than objective information.
*   **Ambiguity Effect:** The tendency to avoid options for which the probability of a favorable outcome is unknown.
*   **Anchoring Bias:** Relying too heavily on the first piece of information encountered (the "anchor") when making decisions.
*   **Attentional Bias:** The tendency for our perceptions to be affected by our recurring thoughts.
*   **Authority Bias:** The tendency to attribute greater accuracy to the opinion of an authority figure and be more influenced by that opinion.
*   **Automation Bias:** The propensity for humans to favor suggestions from automated decision-making systems and ignore contradictory information made without automation.
*   **Availability Heuristic:** Overestimating the importance of information that is immediately available or easy to recall.
*   **Bandwagon Effect:** The tendency to do or believe things because many other people do or believe the same.
*   **Barnum Effect:** Believing that generic personality descriptions apply specifically to oneself (e.g., horoscopes).
*   **Base Rate Fallacy:** Ignoring general information (base rates) in favor of specific, vivid information.
*   **Belief Perseverance (Backfire Effect):** Maintaining a belief despite being presented with contradictory evidence.
*   **Benjamin Franklin Effect:** Liking someone more after doing them a favor.
*   **Bikeshedding:** Giving disproportionate weight to trivial issues while ignoring complex ones.
*   **Bottom-Dollar Effect:** Transferring negative emotions about being low on funds onto the items purchased with those remaining funds.
*   **Bounded Rationality:** The idea that decision-making is limited by the information available, the cognitive limitations of the mind, and the finite amount of time.
*   **Bundling Bias:** Valuing items purchased in a bundle less than those purchased individually.
*   **Bye-Now Effect:** A tendency to spend more after reading or hearing the word "bye" (due to its phonetic similarity to "buy").
*   **Cashless Effect:** The tendency to spend more when using non-physical payment methods (like credit cards) compared to cash.
*   **Category Size Bias:** Thinking an event is more likely to occur in a large category than a small one.
*   **Choice Overload:** Having a harder time choosing when presented with more options.
*   **Cognitive Dissonance:** The mental discomfort experienced when holding two or more contradictory beliefs or values.
*   **Commitment Bias:** Supporting past ideas or actions even when presented with evidence that they are wrong.
*   **Confirmation Bias:** Favoring information that confirms existing beliefs and ignoring information that contradicts them.
*   **Decision Fatigue:** The deteriorating quality of decisions made by an individual after a long session of decision-making.
*   **Declinism:** The belief that a society or institution is tending towards decline, often viewing the past as better than the future.
*   **Decoy Effect:** Changing preference between two options when a third, less attractive "decoy" is presented.
*   **Disposition Effect:** The tendency to sell assets that have increased in value while keeping assets that have dropped in value.
*   **Distinction Bias:** Viewing options as more distinct when evaluating them simultaneously than when evaluating them separately.
*   **Dunning–Kruger Effect:** People with low ability at a task overestimating their own ability.
*   **Einstellung Effect:** Using a familiar solution to a problem even when a better one exists.
*   **Empathy Gap:** Underestimating the influence of visceral states (e.g., hunger, anger) on one's own or others' behavior.
*   **Endowment Effect:** Valuing an object more highly simply because one owns it.
*   **Extrinsic Incentive Bias:** Attributing others' motivations to external factors (like money) while attributing one's own to internal factors.
*   **False Consensus Effect:** Overestimating the extent to which others agree with us.
*   **Framing Effect:** Making different decisions based on how the information is presented (e.g., as a loss or a gain).
*   **Functional Fixedness:** A cognitive bias that limits a person to using an object only in the way it is traditionally used.
*   **Fundamental Attribution Error:** Overemphasizing personal characteristics and underestimating situational factors when judging others' behavior.
*   **Gambler's Fallacy:** Believing that if something happens more frequently than normal during a given period, it will happen less frequently in the future (or vice versa).
*   **Google Effect:** The tendency to forget information that can be easily found online.
*   **Halo Effect:** Allowing a positive impression of a person in one area to influence opinions in other areas.
*   **Hard-Easy Effect:** Overconfidence in difficult tasks and underconfidence in easy tasks.
*   **Hindsight Bias:** The tendency to see past events as being more predictable than they actually were.
*   **Hot Hand Fallacy:** The belief that a person who has experienced success with a random event has a greater chance of further success in additional attempts.
*   **Hyperbolic Discounting:** Preferring smaller, immediate rewards over larger, later rewards.
*   **IKEA Effect:** Placing a disproportionately high value on products one has partially created.
*   **Identifiable Victim Effect:** The tendency to offer more help when a specific, identifiable person is observed under hardship than a large, vague group.
*   **Illusion of Control:** Overestimating one's ability to influence events that are objectively determined by chance.
*   **Illusion of Transparency:** Overestimating how much others can discern one's internal mental state.
*   **Illusion of Validity:** Overestimating the accuracy of one's judgments or predictions.
*   **Illusory Correlation:** Perceiving a relationship between variables even when no such relationship exists.
*   **Illusory Truth Effect:** Believing information is true simply because it has been repeated many times.
*   **Impact Bias:** Overestimating the length or intensity of future emotional states.
*   **In-group Bias:** Favoring members of one's own group over those in an out-group.
*   **Just-world Hypothesis:** The belief that the world is fundamentally just and that people get what they deserve.
*   **Lag Effect:** Learning is better when the same information is presented over a longer period of time rather than all at once.
*   **Law of the Instrument:** Over-reliance on a familiar tool or skill (e.g., "If all you have is a hammer, everything looks like a nail").
*   **Less-is-Better Effect:** Preferring a smaller or lesser option when evaluated in isolation, even if a larger option is objectively better.
*   **Loss Aversion:** The tendency to prefer avoiding losses to acquiring equivalent gains.
*   **Mental Accounting:** Treating money differently depending on its source or intended use.
*   **Mere Exposure Effect:** Developing a preference for things merely because they are familiar.
*   **Messenger Effect:** Being influenced by the person delivering the message rather than the message itself.
*   **Naive Realism:** The belief that we see the world around us objectively and that those who disagree are uninformed or biased.
*   **Negativity Bias:** Giving more weight to negative experiences or information than positive ones.
*   **Normalcy Bias:** The refusal to plan for, or react to, a disaster which has never happened before.
*   **Observer Expectancy Effect:** When a researcher's cognitive bias causes them to subconsciously influence the participants of an experiment.
*   **Omission Bias:** Judging harmful actions as worse than harmful inactions (omissions).
*   **Optimism Bias:** Overestimating the likelihood of positive events and underestimating the likelihood of negative events.
*   **Ostrich Effect:** Avoiding negative financial or personal information by "burying one's head in the sand."
*   **Outcome Bias:** Judging a decision based on its eventual outcome rather than the quality of the decision at the time it was made.
*   **Overjustification Effect:** A decrease in intrinsic motivation for an activity after being rewarded for it.
*   **Peak-end Rule:** Judging an experience based on how it felt at its peak and at its end, rather than the total sum of the experience.
*   **Pessimism Bias:** Overestimating the likelihood of negative outcomes.
*   **Planning Fallacy:** Underestimating the time, costs, and risks of future actions while overestimating the benefits.
*   **Present Bias:** The tendency to settle for a smaller reward today rather than waiting for a larger reward in the future.
*   **Primacy Effect:** The tendency to remember the first items in a list better than the middle items.
*   **Projection Bias:** Overestimating how much our future selves will share our current preferences or feelings.
*   **Recency Effect:** The tendency to remember the most recently presented items best.
*   **Regret Aversion:** Making decisions to avoid the possibility of feeling regret later.
*   **Representativeness Heuristic:** Judging the probability of an event by how much it resembles a prototype.
*   **Restraint Bias:** Overestimating one's ability to show restraint in the face of temptation.
*   **Rosy Retrospection:** Remembering the past as being more positive than it actually was.
*   **Salience Bias:** Focusing on information that is more prominent or emotionally striking while ignoring less remarkable information.
*   **Self-serving Bias:** Attributing successes to internal factors and failures to external factors.
*   **Social Norms:** The unwritten rules of behavior that are considered acceptable in a group or society.
*   **Source Confusion:** Forgetting where a memory or piece of information came from.
*   **Spotlight Effect:** Overestimating how much others notice our appearance or behavior.
*   **Status Quo Bias:** A preference for the current state of affairs.
*   **Survivorship Bias:** Concentrating on the people or things that "survived" a process and inadvertently overlooking those that did not.
*   **Sunk Cost Fallacy:** Continuing an endeavor as a result of previously invested resources (time, money, effort) even when it is no longer rational.
*   **Zero Risk Bias:** The preference for reducing a small risk to zero over a greater reduction in a larger risk.
